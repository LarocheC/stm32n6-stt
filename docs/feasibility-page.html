Citrinet-256 maps to the NPU
with zero software epochs

A push-to-talk English captioner running entirely on an STM32N6: microphone to log-mel on the Cortex-M55, a fixed-shape int8 CTC encoder on Neural-ART, greedy decode and text on the LCD. This is the go/no-go, taken before any firmware was written.

GO at an 8-second window — not the 4 seconds originally planned
ST Edge AI Core v4.0.1-20581 stt_en_citrinet_256_gamma_0_25 ~10M params · 9.7 MB int8 no board measurements yet

The question that mattered

The plan rested on one assumption: that Citrinet's depthwise and grouped 1-D convolution stack would reach Neural-ART hardware. Neural-ART is fundamentally a 2-D convolution engine. Rank-3 tensors, groups=256 depthwise convolutions and squeeze-excitation blocks are all places where a 2-D engine plausibly falls back to the Cortex-M55 — and on this part, latency is epoch-bound, so every fallback is expensive.

It maps. Completely.

The graph is 503 nodes of exactly seven operator types — 282 Conv (107 of them grouped), 130 Relu, 23 ReduceMean, 23 Sigmoid, 23 Mul, 21 Add, 1 Transpose — at rank 3 throughout. No control flow, no attention, no Softmax, no dynamic MatMul.

for contrast

The Whisper-tiny encoder, measured on this same board, ran at 10,935 ms — 184 of its 391 epochs in software, and 16.4 MB of activations spilled to PSRAM. Citrinet at 8 s is 628 epochs, none in software, entirely on-chip. That gap is the whole argument for a convolutional CTC encoder over an attention model on this part.

Where the 8-second window lives

cpuRAM2 19.5% of 1024 KB 200 KB
npuRAM6 94.9% of 448 KB 425 KB
octoFlash 8.7% of 112 MB 9.728 MB

Activations sit on-chip; weights stream from external flash. That split is what makes the utterance-based design work. Doubling the window from 4 s to 8 s costs +23.7% of scheduled cycles, not 2×, because the 9.7 MB weight sweep is paid once per utterance rather than per frame. Cost per second of captured audio falls from 18.4 ms at 4 s to 11.4 ms at 8 s.

The scheduler estimates 91.2 ms for the 8-second graph at 1 GHz, at 43% of its own ideal. That is a compiler estimate of compute and memory traffic; it excludes epoch-transition overhead, and nothing on this machine has previously streamed 10 MB of weights. The honest band is 100–250 ms until the board says otherwise.

What the draft plan got wrong

Four seconds was the worst decision in it

Measured on 150 natural-length LibriSpeech utterances (median 5.55 s), scoring each window's output against the full spoken reference. This is truncation, not misrecognition — but a user cannot tell the difference, and reads it as "it cannot understand me."

Only 28.3% of dev-clean utterances are 4 seconds or shorter.

Twelve seconds is not simply better, which is why 8 s wins rather than the longest window that fits. On short utterances, padding to a long window costs accuracy — per-feature normalisation statistics computed over a mostly-silent buffer drift. On 30 utterances under 3 seconds, WER was 6.15% at exact length, 6.15% in an 8 s window, and 8.21% in a 12 s window. Eight seconds is the only window with no measurable padding penalty and 84% word coverage.

There was no gain stage, and that is the silent failure

would have shipped broken

The DK's microphone puts conversational speech at roughly −54 dBFS. At that level 97.9% of mel-filter outputs fall below NeMo's log-zero guard, the logarithm saturates, per-feature normalisation amplifies what is left, and word error rate goes 5.83% → 35.28% — while every log reports a perfectly clean NPU run.

Peak-normalising the captured buffer to 0.9 restores 5.83% exactly. But the gain has to be applied in the PDM/MDF decimator: applied after int16 truncation, the same fix only recovers to 10.45%.

Three smaller corrections

  • The mel frontend was the wrong thing to fear. The plan flagged NeMo preprocessing parity as the top risk. Ablating pre-emphasis, window periodicity, mel scale, power vs magnitude, log base, centring, even swapping per-feature normalisation for a single global one — every one moves WER by under a point. Only the log floor matters, and it is worth 25 points.
  • The named fallback is dead. QuartzNet-15x5 needs 2,366,976 B of workspace for a single k=75 layer — 157% of the entire pool — and fails to compile on-chip. It has fifteen such layers. The real fallback is the same checkpoint at T=400, already compiled at 73.7 ms.
  • Touch is not available. The GT911 driver ships in neither ST Getting-Started package, so "hold to speak on the touchscreen" is not buildable as scoped. Use the USER1 button. Separately, the audio package is strictly headless — the whole LCD stack has to be grafted in from the vision package.

Work plan

Ordered so the cheapest question is asked first, and so the first thing that can contradict desk research is reached in about one developer-day. Gates 0–2 need neither the board nor a new compile.

00

Freeze the artifact set — 1 h, done

Scripts, graphs, tokenizer and compile evidence out of scratch storage and under version control.

01

WER at the shipped window, fp32 and int8 — 3 h

Current evidence at 4 s: 5.60% → 6.09%, frame-argmax agreement 0.9649. Stop if int8 costs more than 2 points. First verify the calibration and evaluation splits are genuinely disjoint — that check was deferred and never done.

02

Recompile against the firmware's memory map — 30 min

ST's audio application places external flash at 0x70180000; the screening pool used 0x71000000. Left unreconciled, the weights land at the wrong offset and the network reads garbage while the epoch table still looks perfect.

03

Build and flash the unmodified ST audio app board

Prove the toolchain path with ST's own known-good binary before putting a model in the loop. Read the OTP fuse state first — blowing them is permanent.

04

Swap in Citrinet, feed it a canned feature vector board

Ignore the microphone entirely. Compare on-device token IDs against host ONNX Runtime. This is the first point where silicon can contradict desk research, and it arrives roughly one developer-day in.

05

Log-mel frontend on the M55 board — 2–3 d

With the gain stage and the log-floor telemetry built in from the first line, not retrofitted. Stop if guard occupancy stays above 20% after gain — that means the gain sits in the wrong place in the chain.

06

Greedy CTC and detokeniser board — 0.5 d

Argmax over 1025 int8 logits × 100 frames, collapse repeats, drop blank, concatenate pieces. Roughly 50 lines — both scales are per-tensor, so no dequantisation is needed.

07

LCD graft board — 1–1.5 d

Deliberately last: it is the one task that cannot fail in a way that invalidates the model.

Eight to eleven developer-days total, with the risk front-loaded into gates 0–4 — about a day and a half combined.

Top risks

  1. Gain staging against the log-zero guard. Silent and catastrophic; absent from the original plan. Detect by instrumenting guard occupancy on the first frontend commit, and refuse to invoke the model when more than 20% of bins sit at the floor.
  2. ST int8 is not ONNX Runtime int8. Every accuracy figure here is ORT QDQ semantics. Prior work on this board recorded ST diverging at cosine 0.996 with a systematic +0.23 mean bias. Detected at gate 4.
  3. Babble noise — the room the demo is given in. At 5 dB SNR: white 22.3%, pink 15.3%, babble 60.1%. Competing speech is the one noise type that destroys it, and demos happen in rooms full of talking people. "Hold the board near your mouth" is a legitimate and free mitigation.
  4. Grouped convolution mapping is undocumented. The word "group" appears zero times in ST's operator support table. All 107 grouped convolutions reaching hardware is an empirical property of this exact compiler build with no vendor commitment — pin the toolchain and make the 628-epoch compile a regression gate.
  5. OTP fuses, and two flash workflows that overwrite each other. Fuse blowing is permanent and this board's state is unverified. Flashing the demo also overwrites the external-flash weights the measurement harness uses.

Only silicon settles these

  • Real inference latency. 91.2 ms is a scheduler estimate that excludes epoch-transition overhead. Honest band 100–250 ms.
  • On-device int8 fidelity. No amount of host work resolves ST-versus-ORT quantisation arithmetic.
  • M55 log-mel cost. Nobody on this machine has measured any on-device audio frontend — every prior deployment fed the NPU from a host over serial. A ~40× win is available: the filterbank is 2.4% non-zero, so sparse triangular filters cost ~500 MAC/frame against 20,560 for a dense matmul.
  • The microphone's real acoustic behaviour. Every degradation measured here is simulated — synthetic impulse responses, one real 0.1 s RIR, corpus-derived babble.

Every WER figure on this page is LibriSpeech dev-clean read speech through a simulated channel. The checkpoint's training set is broad — 7,000+ hours including Fisher, Switchboard, WSJ and Common Voice — so spontaneous desk speech is less out-of-distribution than one might assume. But no number here was produced by a human talking to this board.

Compile figures are ST Edge AI Core v4.0.1-20581 against stt_en_citrinet_256_gamma_0_25 (CC-BY-4.0), quantised static QDQ int8 on real speech. One adversarial check of the memory gate failed to return; its question was answered instead by three real compiles against ST's audio-pool geometry.
